Price Check

Search a medical procedure and see an honest best guess of what it costs, before anyone makes you pay for a consultation to find out.

Beta · For: Anyone trying to find out what a procedure will cost.

Open Price Check →

Most doctors’ offices and hospitals won’t give a price over the phone. Price Check gives you a starting number: a best guess for the total price of a procedure, the range most people fall in, and what each part of the bill is.

Where the numbers come from

Medicare publishes its price for every procedure: the doctor’s fee in every state, the fee for a surgery center, and the fee for a hospital outpatient department. Those are real payments, not list prices. Private insurers pay more than Medicare, and researchers have measured how much more: about 143% of Medicare’s price for doctors, 170% for surgery centers and 279% for hospital outpatient departments.

Price Check multiplies each part of the bill by those figures, and every estimate shows the arithmetic. The best guess uses the average. For doctors and hospitals, the likely range runs from the lowest to the highest figure the studies found. No study gives a range for surgery centers, so that range is our own assumption, and the page says so. Every source is listed at the bottom of the page, quoted in its own words.

Search by procedure or by doctor

Search for a procedure (“cataract”), a condition (“sciatica”) or a kind of doctor (“dermatologist”). Searching a kind of doctor gives you their price menu: the procedures they commonly do, each with a best guess and a range. Every estimate ends with what to say when you call for a real price.

For scans, where it’s done changes the price more than anything else, so MRI, CT and heart ultrasound estimates show both an imaging center and a hospital.

What it can’t tell you

  • Your share. The estimate is the total price. What you owe depends on your deductible and coinsurance.
  • Your city, your insurer, your doctor. Prices vary a lot within a state. Use the range, not just the best guess.
  • Extras. Lab work, drugs, implants and follow-up visits are usually billed separately. Each estimate says what it leaves out.
  • Cosmetic prices. Procedures done for appearance are priced on a cash market that Medicare data doesn’t see.

It is a way to know whether a quote is reasonable, not a quote.

Disclaimer

Everything Price Check shows is for information only. It is not medical, financial or legal advice, and What Works? does not guarantee that the estimates are accurate, complete, or right for your situation. Always confirm a price directly with the doctor, hospital or facility, and contact your insurance plan about your deductible, out-of-pocket maximum, copay and coinsurance.
