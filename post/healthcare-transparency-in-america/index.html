Healthcare Transparency in America

A person shouldn't have to have an undergraduate degree in public health just to understand how to get help.

By Josh · November 15, 2026

Built with Price Check · try it yourself →

Healthcare transparency in America has always been a complicated topic for a litany of fairly irritating reasons. Not the least of which is how complicated the process of trying to monetize medicine is in general. It’s not like selling something, where there is a physical object that a person can look at; heck, they might even be able to try it out before dropping their hard earned simoleons on whatever newfangled doodad the market has to offer. Healthcare providers, and the people who make their living by profiting off the American medical system, know this. It’s a facet of their industry that they’re not eager to change, so reluctant in fact that the general public has no clue what the going rate for any kind of procedure is.

In fact, most Americans haven’t the foggiest clue how the healthcare system works at all.1 To be quite frank with you, that’s fine. A person shouldn’t have to have an undergraduate degree in public health just to understand how to get help. A person shouldn’t have to have multiple contingency plans in case the initial plan to get help doesn’t work. A person shouldn’t be faced with a complicated network of hucksters and conmen just to try and find a place that might be able to offer them help.

What I’m describing are just a few scenarios that people who want to make use of the American medical system have to deal with: unending bureaucracy, outright obfuscation, and to top it all off, condescension when you don’t smile and thank them for treating you like chopped liver. It’s enough to make you grow grey hairs, or at least just give up on the whole process because trying to manage on your own has to be easier than trying to deal with any of this… right?

I don’t think so. I don’t think that we have to tolerate a broken system, when the tools for beginning to create accessibility to care are waiting for us to make use of them. Already there has been legislation in the direction of healthcare transparency. Acts that require healthcare providers to at least provide a reasonable pathway to some kind of information about how much something will cost you.23 These are steps in the right direction, but from my research I’ve found that a lot of end users are dissatisfied with the experience of these services4 and these people are still having trouble trying to get an estimate5 for a simple cosmetic blepharoplasty.

With this in mind, the team at the What Works? News Network have cooked up a little prix fixe menu of today’s top procedures: generally organized by specialist, these procedure estimations are provided in an easily searchable fashion to help people understand what the ballpark cost of the procedure will be, where we’re getting that information, and how to get a proper estimate from a specialist near them.
